## Context

G2 item 8 turns the four merged pre-G2 contracts and the item 7 domain into wire schemas. G1 left `@taskelith/contracts` with two SDLC documents, a generator for their types and validators, and `make contract-compatibility`. Decision D3 (a) fixes per-worker command subjects and observation streams, D9 (a) per-worker broker credentials, D10 (a) the bootstrap operator credential, and D12 (a) launch authorization over HTTPS. Item 2's JetStream spike is still running, so no subject naming is measured yet.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** the messages of every command, observation, confirmation, and report that the contracts name; the HTTP operations of the plan row; a protocol version with which a worker fails closed; wire enumerations equal to the domain vocabularies.

**Non-Goals:** transport, streams, consumers, HTTP servers, persistence, credential content, and worker or reconciliation logic, which belong to items 11 to 21. Also out: what an offer executes (G3) and the delegation request (capacity reservation section 7), whose harness side is G3.

## Decisions

1. **New documents instead of extending the SDLC ones.** The execution module owns its wire interface, as the SDLC module owns its own. Extending `sdlc.asyncapi.json` would also be breaking under the check: adding a message and a schema to it was measured with `@asyncapi/diff` at the pinned version, and both additions are unclassified, which the check counts as breaking. A new document is absent at the merge base and passes. The SDLC documents are unchanged.
2. **One vocabulary across documents.** The generator lists the documents with their kind and merges their component schemas and paths into one HTTP and one message bundle. `checkHttpSchema` and `checkMessageSchema` therefore keep their API. A schema name with two definitions, or a path defined twice, fails generation. The package test, which before compared the two SDLC documents, now compares every pair of documents. The bundle identifiers drop the `sdlc-` prefix, because the bundles now hold every document; they are internal to the package.
3. **Commands reuse `CommandMetadata`.** Commands keep the section 9 fields of the G1 envelope unchanged. Each execution command narrows it with `allOf`, requiring `attemptId`, `workerId`, and `ownershipGeneration` and fixing `commandType`. The protocol version is `payload.protocolVersion`: the shared metadata rejects unknown properties, and changing it would be a breaking change of the SDLC document.
4. **Observations have their own envelope.** The journal contract makes the producer the pair `(workerId, journalId)` and the identity a deterministic `observationId`. G1's `EventMetadata` has one `producerId` and rejects added properties. `ObservationMetadata` therefore carries the pair and the sequence; it keeps the attempt and ownership generation of section 9.
5. **Confirmation on the command subject.** The trust model gives a worker exactly its own command subject and its own observation subject, so the confirmation travels on the command subject. It is not a command: it has no `commandId` and is not journaled in the inbox, because it is cumulative and idempotent.
6. **Inventory outside the sequence.** The inventory is a snapshot taken on every reconnect and can be rebuilt, so it is not journaled. `throughSequence` orders it after the observations it reflects. Capacity declarations and admission pressure change state the scheduler relies on, so they are sequenced observations and keep their order.
7. **Protocol version and window.** The version is an integer, so that a worker compares it without parsing. `VersionedCommand` is the part that keeps its meaning across versions. An unsupported offer is refused with `protocol-not-supported`, which ends the assignment and releases its reservation. That needs the reason in the scheduling domain's `REFUSAL_REASONS` and in the capacity contract's table. Other commands are rejected by `execution.command.rejected`. The control plane supports the current and the previous version. This is the author's proposal: one previous version lets workers be upgraded after the control plane without a drain.
8. **Launch authorization outcomes.** Status 200 carries every decision recorded for an `operationId`, the retryable rejections `quarantined` and `unavailable` included, because each is final for its operation (launch authorization section 6). Status 503 means no decision was reached, and the worker repeats the same `operationId`. The grant is flat, with `ttlMilliseconds` and `skewMilliseconds` as the domain's launch parameters. `reportsNotStarted` comes from the domain's `LaunchRequest`, where re-authorizing an execution needs it. Only non-retryable reasons appear in `LaunchRejectionReason`, the observation's reason, because only those are reported (section 9).
9. **Task result is the domain outcome fact.** `readTaskResult` returns `pending` or the fields of `TaskOutcomeFact`, the `AttemptOutcome` with its `cancelled`, `ownershipGeneration`, and `endedAt` included, plus the task's evidence records. This keeps item 16 a serialization of the domain.
10. **Evidence.** Publication is one multipart request of the record and its content; the producer chooses `evidenceId`, so that a retry stores nothing twice. The fields follow architecture section 8: producer, task, attempt, run, input artifacts, code revision, validator, outcome, digest, and capture time. The producer is the authenticated worker, never a field. Content is identified by its SHA-256 digest, and download is a separate operation that never serves content that fails its digest.
11. **Credentials.** All three schemes are bearer schemes whose content items 13 and 16 define; enrollment returns both credentials as opaque secrets. Adding a security requirement to the SDLC operations is breaking for `sdlc.openapi.json`, so item 16 makes that change with its version.
12. **Subjects.** The addresses follow D3 (a) and the trust model's permission rule, with the installation as the first segment. Item 2 may still change them; the coordinator relays any conflict.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Item 2 may report a subject, header, or size fact that conflicts with section 2 of the contract page. Mitigation: the documents are at 0.1.0, and no consumer exists before item 12.
- Rules across fields stay in prose, because JSON Schema cannot state them. Examples: `skew` less than `ttl`, `retryable` only with admission pressure, and spawn failure with `never-spawned`. Items 12, 17, 20, and 21 must check them, and the contract page lists them.
- The merged vocabulary forbids two documents from using one name for different schemas. This is intended, and the generator names the conflict.

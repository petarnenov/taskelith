## Context

The decisions come from the owner's design conversation of 2026-10-25 about how the finished product is meant to be used: one intent, a full SDLC cycle, implementation by gates, a generated blueprint with a dependency map, and automatic dispatch that follows the map. They also come from the owner's governance choice: a three-consul council approves everything up to the project's integration branch, only the owner promotes into `main`, and a human is called only when the council cannot convene.

Four existing rules bound the record. Native framework artifacts are the source of truth for specification content, and PostgreSQL stores state rather than competing writable copies. A changed artifact revision invalidates unused admissions. The knowledge graph is advisory and rebuildable, so graph availability cannot gate execution. Auto-merge is the integration executor and merges one change at a time per target branch, and an agent cannot authorize its own integration.

## Goals / Non-Goals

Goals: record each owner decision once, in two ADRs with different consequences; make the conflicting or adjacent rules point at them; state the obligations that keep G3 and G4 compatible with the later introduction; and keep the record honest that nothing is implemented or authorized.

Non-goals: implementing the campaign model, the blueprint manifest, the dispatcher, the branch ladder, the council, decision records, or the communication plugin; changing the frozen SDLC framework port; choosing the manifest's concrete file format or schema; selecting additional harnesses; changing the release or deployment policy.

## Decisions

- **Two ADRs, not one.** The campaign model is domain and architecture; the council is a governance delegation with amendments to ADR 0008 and the trust model. Separate records keep each decision's consequences reviewable on their own.
- **Git stays canonical, PostgreSQL mirrors, Neo4j projects.** The blueprint is framework-neutral and committed to the repository, generated by the plugin from the parent proposal. This follows the existing rule that native artifacts are the source of truth and avoids making the graph a competing writable store. Neo4j is the owner's requested projection store; it sits behind a port with an in-memory implementation and a PostgreSQL fallback, and readiness never reads it, so an unavailable or stale projection cannot dispatch or block work.
- **Readiness instead of priority.** The owner described priority as the dependency graph. A scalar priority would be a second ordering rule that can contradict the graph, so it is not introduced. Graph readiness, every dependency integrated, is necessary but not sufficient: a node also needs a current admission, passed gates, no applicable hold, and a capacity reservation. Operator `order` edges and holds cover the human overlay, and they only add constraints. Integration is defined per node: a change into its integration target, a campaign when all its changes are integrated and its gates passed, an intent when its promotion merge completed.
- **Overrides preserve dependencies.** A hold covers the held node, everything it contains, and everything that depends on either; it never covers a prerequisite, and it never covers a sibling that is neither contained nor dependent, though a dependent sibling waits because of its dependency. A hold that would block a node the operator already requested or queued is rejected with that node named, and a request to run a node under a held ancestor fails with the ancestor named. Containment is not a dependency, so a held campaign never blocks its sibling. This is the owner's rule that a person must not be able to forbid something that another thing they want depends on.
- **One intent branch.** Changes merge into the intent's integration branch; the intent is promoted into the project's integration branch through its own gate. This keeps one serial merge stream per target branch, reuses the conflict flow and its three-attempt budget, and keeps `main` owner-only: no integrator, auto-merge setting, or council approval authorizes that merge.
- **Parent approval covers generated work.** The owner chose this to keep a campaign affordable. Verification and independent review stay per change, and a departure from the blueprint returns to the parent, so the delegation does not become an unapproved scope change.
- **Council: majority first, precedence only on a split.** Three discussion cycles, then 2:1. A 1:1:1 split is decided by the configured precedence ranks, whose owner-approved default is Claude, then Codex, then OpenCode, set at appointment. The method is recorded as the owner's rule, including its consequence that the first seat decides a split while the other two decide only together. A decision needs all three seats; an unavailable seat escalates the question to a human until the owner decides a degraded mode, which needs its own decision.
- **Standing approval, not a vote per change.** A council vote costs up to nine agent runs. The approval of an intent is the standing approval for the specification of its generated changes, for the ratification of their verification and independent-review outcomes, and for their merge into the intent's integration branch; each use is recorded as a decision record bound to the change's revisions and evidence, and it stops applying to that change on a stale revision, a failed or missing gate, a blueprint departure, or a consul's request for a vote. A use applies the intent decision mechanically and is not a new deliberation, so no consul votes on their own implementation; any actual vote follows the independence rule. Without this, a large campaign needs a vote per change.
- **Revocable delegation.** Autonomy is a project setting. The owner can disable the council at any time; recorded decisions remain history, and approvals fall back to the human roles. Without a kill switch, delegating integration to agents would be irreversible.
- **The council arrives with the third harness.** Claude Code is G3, Codex G4, OpenCode later. A partial bench is not introduced, because it would change either the three-harness rule or the majority rule; until the three harnesses are admitted, human approvals apply. The full model therefore follows those admissions and a separate owner authorization; no delivery gate is fixed here.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Parallel changes raise merge contention on the intent branch.** Mitigation: serial merges per target branch, the existing conflict flow with its three-attempt budget, and per-change verification and review.
- **The blueprint manifest is a new contract.** Neither framework has a native graph between changes, so the platform owns a normalized format that must be versioned and validated. Mitigation: the plugin generates it from native artifacts, the platform validates cycles and reachability, and a changed manifest is a new artifact revision that invalidates unused admissions.
- **Delegated authority to agents can produce a bad merge.** Mitigation: the council cannot waive evidence, approve its own work, change policy, release a hold, or touch `main` or release; every decision is audited and revocable.
- **Autonomy hides cost.** A wide graph runs only as wide as the account slots and nested budgets allow; the council itself consumes slots. Mitigation: standing approval, explicit budgets per level, and eligibility rules from the account pools.
- **A stale projection could mislead people.** The graph view in Neo4j may lag. Mitigation: it is a projection, readiness never reads it, and the UI labels its freshness.

## Migration and authorization

No migration: nothing is implemented, so there is no in-flight work to convert. This change authorizes no implementation. When the owner authorizes it, the campaign model and the council become work items of that gate's plan, tracked through the selected SDLC framework. Until then, the forward-compatibility obligations apply to the G3 and G4 designs: approval behind a decision-body port, an integration branch in project settings, readiness computed from canonical state, aggregated board and inbox read models, a versioned plugin operation for the manifest, nested budgets, per-session model and effort on the harness port, and decision records from the first approval.

## Context

Since about 2026-10-27 11:07 local time the coordinator pastes into every brief the review checklist of `make findings ARGS="checklist --capability <a,b> --files <globs>"`: the most frequent archived finding categories for the task's capabilities and files, and a "Record quality" section for findings in the change records. The coordinator (Niki) asked on 2026-10-27, with owner priority, to measure whether the checklist reduces the findings it warns about. Three things were missing: a record of which checklist went into which brief, a baseline to compare with, and a normalization by change size. The coordinator's decisions 1 to 4 of the brief are binding; the choices below fill in what they leave open.

## Decisions

1. **The record, `checklist.json`.** Every change stores the checklist of its brief in its change directory, next to `findings.jsonl`, as one pretty-printed JSON object that `docs/review-findings/checklist.schema.json` defines:
   - `change`; `generatedAt`, the UTC time from `Date.prototype.toISOString`; `devCommit`, the full SHA of the merge base of `HEAD` and `origin/dev`, which is the dev commit whose archive the worktree holds;
   - `query`: `capabilities`, `files`, and `top` exactly as the invocation gave them after list splitting;
   - optional `size`: `S`, `S-M`, `M`, `M-L`, or `L`, as the brief gives it;
   - `review` and `recordQuality`, each with `categories` (the ranked `category` and `count` pairs the text prints) and `baseline` (decision 5).
   The schema refers to the finding schema's `change`, `category`, `identifier`, and `text` definitions, so the records cannot drift apart, and rejects unknown properties. Example titles and descriptions are left out: the text output has them, and the metric needs only the categories.
2. **One command saves it.** `make findings ARGS="checklist <brief arguments> --save <change> [--size <size>]"` prints the same text as without `--save`, then writes `openspec/changes/<change>/checklist.json`. It refuses a change that is not an active change directory and an existing file, so the record stays the one made at plan time; the write uses the exclusive `wx` flag. `--size` needs `--save`, and `--json` and `--save` exclude each other. `checklist --json` prints `{ query, review, recordQuality }` with each item's category, description, count, and example.
3. **Cutoff: the `.openspec.yaml` created date.** Validation requires `checklist.json` in an active change whose `.openspec.yaml` `created` date, which `openspec new change` writes, is on or after `CHECKLIST_REQUIRED_FROM` = `2026-10-28` in `scripts/checklist-record.ts`, and in an active change without a readable created date, which cannot show that it predates the rule. The date is the day after this change was developed: it is deterministic, needs no git history, and exempts the changes that parallel workers created on 2026-10-27 before this rule existed. The cost is that a change created later on 2026-10-27 is exempt too. Archived changes never need the file and are not rewritten. Every `checklist.json`, active or archived, is checked against the schema, its directory's change, and the category vocabulary; a problem summary that names a `checklist.json` also names its schema.
4. **No backfill.** Earlier briefs exist only in the sessions' chat history, not in the repository, so their checklists cannot be recovered reliably, and recomputing them now would rank a later archive. No archived change gets a `checklist.json`. This change records its own, generated with `--save` from the brief's invocation `checklist --files scripts/**` and the brief's size `S-M`.
5. **Baseline.** At save time, each section stores the archived findings of its population and how many of them have a listed category, all and Medium or worse (`critical`, `high`, or `medium`). The review population is every archived finding outside `openspec/changes/` (code findings), the record quality population every archived finding under it (record findings), both whatever the query. The expected share is `inChecklist / findings`: how often the listed categories occurred in the archive the checklist was ranked from, so the "before that change" of the brief is exactly the archive at `devCommit`. A query-matched population was rejected: the checklist lists its top categories, which often cover all of that population, so the expected share would be close to 100% by construction. The baseline is stored, not recomputed, because archive directories carry only a date, and on 2026-10-27 dozens of changes were archived on one day.
6. **Observed shares.** The effect counts only pass-1 findings, of every artifact, because later passes review fixes and their scope is the earlier passes' findings. Review compares pass-1 code findings with the review list; record quality compares pass-1 record findings with the record quality list. Each change shows `inChecklist of findings listed`, the observed share, and the expected share of its baseline, also when it has no findings. A share has no value when its denominator is 0.
7. **Aggregation by archive date.** The archive date is the `<date>-` prefix of the archive directory. A day's observed share is the sum of its listed findings over the sum of its findings; its expected share is the findings-weighted mean of its changes' expected shares, `Σ findings × expected / Σ findings`, so a change with more findings weighs more, as it does in the observed share. A change without findings in a section does not weigh in; if a change with findings has no expected share, the day has none.
8. **Size normalization.** The task size of the brief is preferred, because it is what the coordinator planned, it exists at plan time, and the record already carries it. Size points: S 1, S-M 1.5, M 2, M-L 2.5, L 3, a linear scale with the in-between sizes halfway. A change without a recorded size is normalized per 100 lines added or removed outside `openspec/changes/` between `devCommit` and the commit that added the archived `checklist.json` (`git diff --shortstat`, renames off). A day shows both rates over the changes that have them; when git cannot find the archive commit, for example before the archive is committed, the size is reported as unknown.
9. **Module split.** Another worker changes `scripts/findings.ts`, `scripts/review-findings.ts`, `scripts/review-passes.ts`, and `scripts/reviewer-statistics.ts` next, so the new code lives in its own modules: `scripts/checklist-record.ts` (pure record rules, cutoff, baseline, validation), `scripts/checklist-effect.ts` (pure metric and its text), `scripts/checklist-commands.ts` (the `checklist` and `effect` commands over loaded records, and the single file write), and `scripts/checklist-environment.ts` (the clock and git, replaceable in tests). `scripts/findings.ts` only parses the new options, loads `checklist.json` and the created date, adds the validation problems, and dispatches; the text `checklist` output moved unchanged into `scripts/checklist-commands.ts`.
10. **Requirement length.** The added requirement bodies are 471 ("Checklist record"), 446 ("Checklist effect"), and 351 ("Checklist record in the task flow") characters, under the 500 that strict validation accepts after archive.

## Risks

- The record quality list ranks seven categories by default, which on 2026-10-27 cover every archived record finding, so its expected share is 100% and its observed share cannot exceed it; the record quality comparison only becomes informative when the list is shorter than the category set, for example with `--top`. The review comparison does not have this problem.
- Few changes per day and few pass-1 findings per change make the shares noisy; the output gives the counts with every share so that readers can judge.
- Lines changed include the lines of any `dev` merge into the branch after `devCommit`, because the diff runs from `devCommit` to the archive commit.
- A worker who regenerates the checklist later than the brief gets counts of a newer archive; `devCommit` and `generatedAt` record which archive it ranked.

# Design: implement-session-timeline

## Context

G3 item 13 ([G3 plan](/docs/g3-plan.md) row 13) builds on the pure domain of item 4 (`define-session-turn-model`, archived 2026-10-28; `packages/modules/execution/src/domain/session-*.ts`) and the [session timeline contract](/docs/contracts/session-timeline.md). The plan row names the PostgreSQL adapter and the application service: turn submission with idempotency keys, request and response records, observation intake, and crash points. The brief (coordinator Niki, 2026-10-29) adds: native requests persisted before display, single consumption, a reused native request ID under another generation rejected, races tested on PostgreSQL, no wire operation beyond 9b and 9c, no new dependency, migrations in their own numbered files, and a split proposal if the work is clearly larger than M-L.

The study for this design found that the contracts written after item 4 put more on item 13 than the plan row states:

- **Session timeline section 9** lists item 13's tests: attempt uniqueness across a task's sessions, a request committed before display, a submission retried across a restart, a response consumed once across concurrent answers, the reconciliation of section 6 at a restart, `recordTurnAcceptanceUnknown` (section 4.4) and `recordTurnRefused` with the state `refused` (section 4.5) "in the domain and the service", and the crash points of each path. Neither operation nor the state `refused` exists in the domain at `62217e7a` (`TURN_STATES` is `queued`, `delivered`, `ended`, `outcome-unknown`), although the living `session-turn-model` spec has both requirements.
- **Control-plane wire contracts section 12** (9b): the service behind section 9 (`submitTurn`, `respondToNativeRequest`, the reads, the output read), the idempotency keys of turns and responses with an HMAC-SHA-256 digest of the unredacted input, redaction of session text before it is recorded, output redacted across chunks with a durable held tail, the native request source that opens the `delivery-acknowledgement` inbox item, and the atomic key check.
- **Worker messages and terminal section 12** (9d): the session messages in the NATS adapter's schema tables, the session commands written in the transactions that make them due with a stored `commandId`, the observations of section 9 applied through the timeline after their recovery epoch check, the classified-operation check before a standing rule answers, and the control plane's protocol window raised to 1 to 2.
- The wire `TurnState` of `control-plane.openapi.json` has no `refused`, and `packages/contracts/test/board-session-contracts.test.ts` asserts it equals `TURN_STATES`.

## Goals and non-goals

Goals of this change (13a; the coordinator accepted the split of "Size and split", answer Q1): the domain completion, the durable timeline store, the application service, and their tests and crash points. Non-goals: the HTTP operations and the inbox source (13b), the worker messages, observation intake from the broker, and the protocol window (13c), the harness host (item 18), the event stream (item 14), the UI, and delegation admission (item 38).

## Decisions

1. **Domain completion** (`domain/session-turns.ts`, `domain/session-records.ts`). `TURN_STATES` gains `refused`; `TurnProgress` gains `{ state: 'refused'; generation }`. `recordTurnAcceptanceUnknown(session, { turnId, generation })` and `recordTurnRefused(session, { turnId, generation })` follow sections 4.4 and 4.5 in their check order. `nextDeliverableTurn` returns nothing while a turn of the running generation is `outcome-unknown` through section 4.4, and skips `refused` turns. `recordTurnOutcome`, `recordRequest`, and `appendOutput` refuse a `refused` turn as the contract states. Unit tests in the new `packages/modules/execution/test/session-turn-reports.test.ts`. The wire `TurnState` of `control-plane.openapi.json` stays without `refused` (answer Q8): `board-session-contracts.test.ts` compares the wire enumeration with a wire list, the domain's `TURN_STATES` without `refused`, and nothing in 13a maps a domain `refused` turn onto the wire.
2. **Store port** (`ports/session-timeline-store.ts`). `SessionTimelineStore.transaction(work)` runs one `SERIALIZABLE` transaction and commits only on `Ok`, as `AccessStore` and `CapacityStore` do. `SessionTimelineTransaction` holds `sessions` (`get`, `add`, `replace` at the stored version, `listByTask`) and `idempotency` (`find` and `record` of a key with its scope, digest, and first outcome). A session's record version is a store concern, not a domain field: the repository returns `{ session, version }`.
3. **One attempt, one session.** The service reads the task's sessions through `listByTask` inside the transaction and passes them to `bindAttempt`. The PostgreSQL adapter also keeps `agent_session_attempts (attempt_id PRIMARY KEY, session_id, task_id)`, so two concurrent bindings of one attempt to two sessions commit at most once even if a predicate read were missed; the unique violation is a `conflict`.
4. **Application service** (`application/session-timeline-service.ts`, the module's first `application` directory, as Access and Scheduling have). One method per contract operation: `openSession`, `bindAttempt`, `endBoundAttempt`, `submitTurn`, `consumeResponse`, and the host's reports (`startGeneration`, `endGeneration`, `deliverTurn`, `recordTurnOutcome`, `recordTurnAcceptanceUnknown`, `recordTurnRefused`, `recordRequest`, `recordDeliveryReport`, `appendOutput`, `recordLimitSignal`, `writeHandoff`, `takeHandoff`), and reads (`readSession`, `nextDeliverableTurn`, `requestsAwaitingAnswer`, `uncertainDeliveries`). Every result shows a session as `SessionView`, the session without its output window, and the service has no output read: output leaves only through the store and the tests until 13b (answer Q5). Each change reads, applies the domain, and writes in one transaction; the service returns only after the commit, so a read never shows an uncommitted request (section 5.1). The service returns the domain's `Result` unchanged; mapping a refusal to "applied without effect" is the observation intake's (13c).
5. **Idempotency by digest.** Redaction loses information, so the service resolves a repeated key before the domain by a keyed digest of the unredacted input (control-plane section 7): a turn's key is scoped to its session and its digest covers the author and the text; a response's key is scoped to the session and the request key and covers the answer, the responder, and the attempt with its ownership generation. The same key with the same digest returns the first outcome (`duplicate`, or the answered request); another digest is `conflict` with the detail `reason` `idempotency-key-reused`. A native request has no client key, so its own key, the native request ID scoped to its generation, holds the digest of its unredacted kind, turn, and text in the same table: a repeat with the same digest changes nothing, and another digest is the `conflict` of contract section 5.1 (review pass 1), so that two texts that redact alike are never taken for one. The digest port `InputDigest` has an HMAC-SHA-256 adapter over `node:crypto` (`HmacInputDigest` of `@taskelith/secrets`). Its key is the file `control-plane/session-input-digest.key` of answer Q4: `SessionInputDigestKeyFile` of `@taskelith/control-plane-store` creates it once through `ControlPlaneNamespace.create` (mode `0600`), and `startSessionTimelineService` of `apps/control-plane` refuses to start when it is missing or not 32 bytes, with a reason that names only its size. A turn's `TurnId` is minted by the caller (contract section 2 rule 6), here through an injected identifier source.
6. **Session text.** The service redacts a turn's text, an answer's text and message, and a request's text through a `SessionTextRedactor` port before the domain records it, after it checked each text against its bound, so that redaction never shortens an overlong text into the bound (review pass 1), with an adapter over `redactSecrets` of `@taskelith/secrets`. Redaction can also lengthen a text past its bound (`pwd=x` becomes `pwd=[redacted]`), so after redaction the service cuts only the tail to the bound (review pass 2, answer M1): a turn's text, a request's text, an output chunk, an answer's message, and a delegation summary to `MAX_HARNESS_TEXT_LENGTH` UTF-16 code units, as the domain counts them, and an input answer to `MAX_INPUT_ANSWER_BYTES` UTF-8 bytes, always on a whole character, never inside a surrogate pair or a UTF-8 sequence. A cut prefix of redacted text holds no secret. Since an input answer is cut in bytes, the service also checks it against `MAX_INPUT_ANSWER_BYTES` before redaction, the bound that control-plane `InputAnswerText` already states, so that only redaction can make it cut. Output redaction across chunks with its durable held tail is 13b (question Q1); until then `appendOutput` redacts each chunk alone, which the design records as a known limit, not as the contract's rule.
7. **Persistence** (`packages/adapters/postgres/src/session-timeline-repositories.ts`, migration `1792933000000_session-timeline.sql`). `agent_sessions (session_id, task_id, version, record)` keeps the session as JSON text without its output entries; `agent_session_output (session_id, position, record)` keeps the retained window, each entry as JSON text so that every string the domain accepts round-trips, trimmed in the same transaction as the append; `agent_session_attempts` of decision 3; `session_idempotency_keys (session_id, scope, key, digest, outcome, PRIMARY KEY (session_id, scope, key))`. The repository reassembles the domain `Session` on read. Storage shape is answer Q2.
8. **Crash points.** `PostgresCrashPoint` gains `SessionTimelineCommit.before` and `SessionTimelineCommit.after`, reached around the commit of every service transaction, as `LaunchAuthorizationCommit` is.

## Crash points and their tests

All crash tests run a child process through `ScenarioProcess` with `crashEnvironment(point)` and `SIGKILL`, as `crash-recovery.integration.test.ts` does, against PostgreSQL 18.6.

| Path | Crash point | Test asserts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `submitTurn` | `before` | No turn and no key stored; the retry with the same key records one turn at position 1 (`recorded`) |
| `submitTurn` | `after` | One turn and its key stored; the retry with the same key and input returns `duplicate`, still one turn |
| `consumeResponse` | `before` | The request is still `open`; the retry consumes it once |
| `consumeResponse` | `after` | The request is `answered`; the retry with the same key returns the answered request, a retry with another key or answer is `conflict` |
| `recordRequest` | `before` | No read lists the request; the host's repeated report records it once |
| `recordRequest` | `after` | `requestsAwaitingAnswer` lists it once; a repeated report changes nothing |
| `endGeneration` (reconciliation) | `before`, `after` | Before: nothing reconciled, a repeat reconciles once. After: `unresolved`, `delivery-uncertain`, and `outcome-unknown` as section 6; a repeat changes nothing |
| `recordTurnAcceptanceUnknown`, `recordTurnRefused` | `after` | The state survives; a repeat returns the session unchanged; `nextDeliverableTurn` follows sections 4.4 and 4.5 after a restart |

"A restart" in these tests is a new process with a new `PostgresDatabase` over the same database (question Q6).

## Test plan

- **Fail-before.** Each test is written before its code, except the down migration test, which a later mutation run checks (`verification.md`, review pass 2). Domain tests run against stub exports that return `internal` ("not implemented") so that they compile and fail by assertion; the service and store tests run against a store whose methods return `internal`; the PostgreSQL tests run before the migration exists. `verification.md` records how each failed, as `wire-git-workspace-into-worker` did.
- **Conformance.** `ports/session-timeline-conformance.ts` runs on `InMemorySessionTimelineStore` in the module's tests and on the PostgreSQL store in `packages/adapters/postgres/test/session-timeline.integration.test.ts`: round-trip of every string the domain accepts, version compare-and-set, `listByTask`, attempt uniqueness, the key table, and output trimming.
- **Races on PostgreSQL** (checklist `concurrency-race`), each with two real transactions synchronized by a barrier inside the work, never a sleep (Test authoring): two submissions with one key and one input (one `recorded`, one `duplicate`, one turn); one key with two inputs (one `recorded`, one `conflict` `idempotency-key-reused`); two keys at once (positions 1 and 2, no gap); two responses to one request (one consumed, the other `conflict`), also with one key (the second returns the first outcome); one attempt bound to two sessions of a task (one bound, one `conflict`); a response racing the generation's end (either answered then `delivery-uncertain`, or `not-found` on an `unresolved` request).
- **Idempotency contract** (checklist `contract-drift`): the same key from another author with the same text is `conflict`, and two inputs that redact to the same text with one key are `conflict`, never `duplicate`.
- **Reused native request ID**: a request of generation 2 reusing generation 1's ID is recorded `open`; a response naming generation 1 fails as that request's state requires, through the service and on PostgreSQL.
- Test titles state exactly what the assertions check (checklist `naming`). Coverage stays at or above 75% of lines, branches, and functions.

## Size and split

Measured against the brief's M-L: the domain completion, the store port with its in-memory adapter and conformance suite, the service with digest and redaction ports, the PostgreSQL adapter and migration, and their tests are about 1,300 lines of source and 1,800 lines of tests: the top of M-L. The obligations that 9b and 9d added each have the same order of size again and need other inputs (items 10 and 11 for project authorization of the HTTP operations, the NATS adapter and the observation consumer for the messages). The item is clearly larger than M-L, so the design proposes:

- **13a `implement-session-timeline`** (this change, M-L): the scope of "Decisions".
- **13b** (M-L): the HTTP operations of control-plane section 9 (`listAgentSessions`, `readAgentSession`, `listAgentSessionTurns`, `listAgentSessionRequests`, `readAgentSessionOutput`, `submitTurn`, `respondToNativeRequest`) over the 13a service, output redaction across chunks with the durable held tail, and the native request source with the `delivery-acknowledgement` item (section 8.1).
- **13c** (M-L): the worker messages of 9d: the schema tables, the session commands written in the transactions that make them due with their stored `commandId`, the observations applied through the 13a service after the recovery epoch check, `endBoundAttempt` from the attempt's outcome fact, the classified-operation check before a standing rule answers, and the control plane's protocol window raised to 1 to 2.

13b and 13c depend on 13a only and can run in parallel; item 18 needs 13c, item 14 needs 13a, and item 15 needs 13b.

## Coordinator questions

**Q1. Split.** Is item 13 split into 13a, 13b, and 13c as "Size and split" proposes? Options: (a) the three-way split; (b) 13a plus one 13b with both the HTTP and the worker messages (L-XL); (c) no split. Recommendation: (a). It amends no contract; the G3 plan row 13 gains the sub-changes, as rows 9 and 16 have them.

**Q2. Storage shape.** The `postgres-state` requirement "Conformant repositories" keeps one JSON record per aggregate, but a session's output window holds up to 10 MiB, which every append would rewrite. Options: (a) the session record without its output entries, plus an `agent_session_output` row per retained entry, trimmed in the append's transaction; (b) one JSON record including the window; (c) normalized tables for turns and requests too. Recommendation: (a), with a `postgres-state` requirement that names the exception. It amends no contract. Every change still loads the whole window; loading it only for output operations is an optimization left for later.

**Q3. Response idempotency key.** The domain's `consumeResponse` has no key, while control-plane section 9 gives every response one, scoped to its request. Options: (a) the service stores it in `session_idempotency_keys` with scope `response:<generation>:<nativeRequestId>`; (b) add a key to the domain's `ResponseSubmission`. Recommendation: (a): the key is a wire and service concern, and the domain stays as reviewed. No contract change.

**Q4. Digest key.** The HMAC-SHA-256 key that "no response or log carries" has no home yet. Options: (a) a 32-byte file created once in the control-plane namespace (`ControlPlaneNamespace`, `control-plane/session-input-digest.key`); (b) a row in PostgreSQL; (c) derive it from an existing installation secret. Recommendation: (a), so that a database dump does not carry it; the store tests use an injected key. Note: after a restore with another key a repeat is answered `idempotency-key-reused`, which is safe (no second effect). It amends no contract; the storage contract lists the file if the coordinator agrees.

**Q5. Redaction in 13a.** Is redaction of turns, answers, and request text in 13a (decision 6), with output redaction across chunks in 13b? Options: (a) yes; (b) all redaction in 13b, 13a records text as given and compares inputs exactly. Recommendation: (a): the digest check of 13a exists because of redaction, and moving it later would change stored records. No contract change; 13a's per-chunk output redaction is a stated limit until 13b.

**Q6. Reconciliation "at a restart".** Section 9 asks for "the reconciliation of section 6 applied at a restart" without naming who triggers it. Options: (a) the host's `session.generation.ended` after the worker's restart, or the attempt's end, applied through the service; tested as a new process over the same database; (b) a control-plane start sweep that ends the generations of ended attempts. Recommendation: (a); the attempt-end trigger is wired in 13c. No contract change.

**Q7. Migration number.** Pepi's 12a adds a migration in parallel. Options: (a) 13a takes `1792933000000_session-timeline.sql` and 12a `1792932900000`; (b) whichever merges second renumbers above the other. Recommendation: (a), told to Pepi now, so neither renumbers after review; both are fresh installs only, so the order check of "Clock-independent migration order" cannot fire.

**Q8. `refused` on the wire.** Adding `refused` to `TURN_STATES` breaks `board-session-contracts.test.ts`, because the wire `TurnState` and the `Turn` variants of `control-plane.openapi.json` lack it although contract section 4.5 defines the state. Options: (a) 13a adds `refused` to `TurnState` and a `Turn` variant with its generation, an additive enum value recorded as `breaking-changes.json` requires; (b) 13b does it, and 13a keeps the test green by a separate constant. Recommendation: (a): it is the drift 9d left, and (b) would add another. It amends the wire document, not a contract page.

**Q9. Who opens a session.** No contract names the caller of `openSession` or who mints `SessionId`. Options: (a) 13a exposes the service operation and the control plane mints the ID; the caller, placement with the 9d `AssignmentSessionBinding`, is 13c's; (b) 13a also wires placement. Recommendation: (a). No contract change.

**Q10. Events for item 14.** Control-plane section 10 assigns event positions after the commit. Options: (a) 13a writes no event record, and item 14 decides its source (for example a change log it adds to the 13a transactions); (b) 13a appends a `session_changes` row per committed change now. Recommendation: (a), to keep 13a tight; item 14 then extends the 13a transactions. No contract change.

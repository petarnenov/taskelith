## Context

The [worker journal contract](/docs/contracts/worker-journal.md) names the commits W1 to W6 (W5s and W5e apart) as crash points, before and after each, and section 13 asks for a crash at each, followed by recovery, with the result of its recovery table. The [supervisor identity contract](/docs/contracts/supervisor-identity.md) names the launch steps CP1 to CP12 (section 4) and asks, in section 13, that a crash of the worker or a supervisor at each gives the result section 6 assigns and never a second spawn. Items 14 and 15 already crash the journal adapter and the supervisor program alone; 24b crashes the composed system. The owner's delegate decided that these scenarios run "through the harness" as test-side compositions of the real modules against the `make` services, with armed crash points, and that the product worker gains no crash seam (24a design decision 6).

## Decisions

1. **The composition.** `apps/control-plane/test/harness-composition.ts` starts, per scenario, a database, a data root, and a broker account of its own on the `make` services, initializes the installation with the administration CLI, and starts the real control plane in the test process with short test timings. The worker is a child process, `fixtures/composed-worker.ts`, that runs the real `workerMain` with `productionDependencies`; it overrides only the free-space and daemon-overhead sensors, which are not under test, the transport timings, and the jitter draw. The scenario submits a planning task through the operator API, as the item 23 smoke does, and the control plane places, offers, and authorizes it. The test-only scripted child appends its process ID to a `spawns` file and runs until the test releases it, at most 30 s: at once where the crash follows its exit (W5e, CP11, CP12), after an adopting supervisor wrote its record at CP9, and otherwise once the restarted worker is online; a child released by its bound fails the scenario.
2. **Crash points without a product seam.** The worker journal adapter already calls an `onCommitPoint` hook at `W<n>.before` and `W<n>.after`, but `startWorker` does not expose it. The worker process registers a `node:module` resolution hook before it loads the worker, so every import of `@taskelith/worker-journal` resolves to `fixtures/armed-journal.ts`, which re-exports the adapter and passes the hook into `openWorkerRoot` and `claimWorkerRoot`. The supervision port of the launch host is wrapped: `prepare` reaches CP1 on entry and CP2 after it wrote `intent`, `start` reaches CP3 on entry and CP4 after the supervisor started, CP10 and CP12, where the worker reads `handle` or `exit` and puts the observation in its outbox, end with the journal commit of the start or exit fact, which holds its outbox row: the armed journal reaches CP10 right after W5s and CP12 right after W5e, and the scenario checks the journaled `started` or `exited` state before the restart. `start` launches `fixtures/armed-supervisor.ts`, the real supervisor program with its existing `crashPoint` option, which reaches CP5 to CP9 and CP11; adopting supervisors stay the product's. `TASKELITH_CRASH_POINT` arms one point in both processes, the harness's `CrashPoints` kills the process that reaches it with `SIGKILL`, and the point is appended to a trace file first, so the test knows which process died where. Two test-side barriers fix an order the scheduler would otherwise choose, and append it to an order trace: at CP4 the worker crashes only once the supervisor's claim is written, so recovery adopts a claimed record; in the D1 windows the worker publishes a sealed execution's `not-started` only after the authority answered another execution's launch request.
3. **Restart.** A crashed worker is started again on the same data root. After a supervisor's crash, the test waits until the supervisor is gone and stops the worker with `SIGTERM`, as a service restart; the next start runs the recovery of journal contract section 5. The restarted worker must log its first reconciliation pass and its broker contact before it is stopped. W6 commits only in a recovery that seals, so its scenarios crash first at W3.after and then at W6 in the recovering worker.
4. **The checks.** After the restart, the test waits for the outcome in the control plane's task: an attempt that ended `exited`, or an execution with `uncertain` evidence. It then stops the worker and waits until the control plane agrees with the worker journal on the state and the `uncertain` evidence of every execution it knows, while the journal's other executions are `not-started` without a grant; it checks one attempt, exactly one execution beyond `not-started`, with exit code 0 and a proven start or with `uncertain`; one launch intent; the number of spawns; and, once the child had time to exit, no live process whose command line names the scenario's directory, which covers the worker, every supervisor, and the child.
5. **Duplicate delivery.** The test reads the offer from the commands stream, which keeps acknowledged messages, and publishes the same bytes again under new broker message identities, as a redelivery after the deduplication window: one to three copies while the child runs and one to three after the result, the counts drawn from the seed. The child is released only after the copies during the execution are acknowledged. After the last copies are acknowledged and the observations stream is drained and quiet for a second, the test reads the task again and checks one launch intent, one execution in the control plane and the journal, attempts unchanged since the result, one spawn, and one result.
6. **Seeds.** The suite takes one seed from `TASKELITH_TEST_SEED` or draws one; each worker run of a scenario seeds its jitter with the seed plus the run's index, and the duplicate counts come from the seed. Identifiers stay random, because an execution identity must never repeat across restarts. Every record carries the seed.
7. **Expected outcomes on macOS.** The tables of journal section 5 and supervisor section 6 give: W1 to W5e, W6, CP1 to CP6, and CP10 to CP12 end `exited` with code 0 and one spawn, after a seal and a new execution where no supervisor claimed; CP7 (a claim, no spawn) and CP8 (a spawn, no handle) end `uncertain`, because macOS has no containment that proves no process; CP9 ends `uncertain` too: the restarted worker adopts the child, an adopting supervisor on macOS only observes, and once the child is gone nothing proves that no descendant lives.
8. **Platform scope.** The scenarios run natively on macOS, where the worker supervises without a containment, and are skipped elsewhere. On Linux the worker needs a delegated cgroup and an execution account, which only the privileged container of `make verify-linux-cgroup` provides, and that container has no network for the `make` services; the supervisor-level crashes at CP5 to CP11 with a containment already run there (item 15). Item 25 runs item 24's scenarios on each Linux matrix entry.
9. **Expected failures for D1.** The evidence run found defect D1 (`verification.md`): a relaunch after `not-started` is rejected with `execution-conflict`, so eleven windows that seal an authorized execution and launch again (W3, W4, and W6 on both sides, CP1, CP2, CP3, CP5, CP6) never reach their outcome. The coordinator decided to keep them as expected failures, not skips: each carries `expectFailure` of `node:test` with D1's signature, which the scenario raises only after it checked D1 exactly: the control plane logged one launch rejection, `execution-conflict`, for an execution E2; the journal holds one intent and two executions, E1 `not-started` and never rejected, and E2 on E1's assignment without a grant, whose only operation is `rejected-final` with `execution-conflict`; the control plane knows only E1, `not-started`, in one attempt; the order trace shows E2's answer before E1's `not-started` was published; and no process is left and nothing spawned. They still record `failed`. Once D1 is fixed they fail, until the fix's change turns them back into ordinary scenarios.
10. **D1 and O1 are fixed elsewhere.** The owner's delegate decided both in away run `20261026T161148Z-opencode-90873`: D1 by option (b), an optional `reportsNotStartedFor` in the launch request that the authority applies before the ownership rule, in one transaction, which changes the launch authorization contract; O1, a live worker that does not notice a supervisor dead before its claim, by (A), a liveness check of an authorized but unclaimed record after a bound. D1 is fixed in `fix-launch-recovery-reauthorization`, which reruns this evidence with seed `2776760789`, and O1 in `fix-unclaimed-launch-liveness`; CP5 may need both to pass; this change touches no product code or contract.

11. **Bounded waits and cleanup.** A scenario's signal combines the test's own and a deadline 30 s before the runner's timeout. Every wait gives up a check or operation after 20 s or once that signal aborts: the database, broker, and HTTP calls of the start and of the checks, the `ps` listings, and each check of a wait. Giving up does not stop every operation: the HTTP calls take the signal, the harness's PostgreSQL clients and its task store carry query and connection timeouts, and `ps` has a timeout, but the in-process administration calls and the system-account registration end only by their own timeouts. What an abandoned operation still yields is disposed of: a late broker connection is closed, a late control plane start is stopped. A worker that does not exit in time is killed. The scenario and its installation share one release stack, each release registered before its acquisition is awaited: the installation's releases, the leftover reaping, and each worker's kill, run last first, also when a start fails. Teardown ignores the aborted signal; the whole cleanup has one deadline of 22 s inside the 30 s reserve, each step at most 8 s of what is left and one more 1 s try once it passed; every step runs, their errors are collected, and the result is recorded only after the cleanup returned. Deleting the scenario's streams accepts only an absent stream and reports every other error after it tried them all.

## Not applicable on this host

- The Linux containment of CP6 (create, admit, remove) and the Linux rows of the recovery table: the scenarios run on macOS, where CP6 is reached as a named point without a containment. Covered at the supervisor level by `make verify-linux-cgroup`; the composition on Linux belongs to item 25.

## Risks and limitations

- The resolution hook replaces the journal module by its specifier; a worker that imported the adapter under another name would escape it. The trace file and the `SIGKILL` status prove that each armed point was reached.
- The launch supervisor of the scenarios is the real program run from a test fixture with test timings, not the worker's built entry; adopting supervisors are the product's.
- The control plane runs in the test process, so a crash of the control plane is out of scope here.

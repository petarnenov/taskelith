## Context

G2 item 8 turns the four merged pre-G2 contracts and the item 7 domain into wire schemas. G1 left `@taskelith/contracts` with two SDLC documents, a generator for their types and validators, and `make contract-compatibility`. Decision D3 (a) fixes per-worker command subjects and observation streams, D9 (a) per-worker broker credentials, D10 (a) the bootstrap operator credential, and D12 (a) launch authorization over HTTPS. Item 2's JetStream spike is still running, so no subject naming is measured yet.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** the messages of every command, observation, confirmation, and report that the contracts name; the HTTP operations of the plan row and the delegation request whose wire form capacity reservation section 7 assigns to this item; a protocol version with which a worker fails closed; wire enumerations equal to the domain vocabularies.

**Non-Goals:** transport, streams, consumers, HTTP servers, persistence, credential content, and worker or reconciliation logic, which belong to items 11 to 21. Also out: what an offer executes and the harness side of delegation, both G3's.

## Decisions

1. **New documents instead of extending the SDLC ones.** The execution module owns its wire interface, as the SDLC module owns its own. Extending `sdlc.asyncapi.json` would also be breaking under the check: adding a message and a schema to it was measured with `@asyncapi/diff` at the pinned version, and both additions are unclassified, which the check counts as breaking. A new document is absent at the merge base and passes. The SDLC documents change only by decision 11.
2. **One vocabulary across documents.** The generator lists the documents with their kind and merges their component schemas and paths into one HTTP and one message bundle. `checkHttpSchema` and `checkMessageSchema` therefore keep their API. A schema name with two definitions in any two documents, HTTP or message, or a path defined twice, fails generation (pass-2 finding 5). The package test, which before compared the two SDLC documents, now compares every pair of documents. The bundle identifiers drop the `sdlc-` prefix, because the bundles now hold every document; they are internal to the package.
3. **Commands reuse `CommandMetadata`.** Commands keep the section 9 fields of the G1 envelope unchanged. Each execution command narrows it with `allOf`, requiring `attemptId`, `workerId`, and `ownershipGeneration` and fixing `commandType`. The protocol version is `payload.protocolVersion`: the shared metadata rejects unknown properties, and changing it would be a breaking change of the SDLC document.
4. **Observations have their own envelope.** The journal contract makes the producer the pair `(workerId, journalId)` and the identity a deterministic `observationId`. G1's `EventMetadata` has one `producerId` and rejects added properties. `ObservationMetadata` therefore carries the pair and the sequence; it keeps the attempt and ownership generation of section 9.
5. **Confirmation on the command subject.** The trust model gives a worker exactly its own command subject and its own observation subject, so the confirmation travels on the command subject. It is not a command: it has no `commandId` and is not journaled in the inbox, because it is cumulative and idempotent.
6. **Inventory outside the sequence.** An omission proves lost history only for an assignment whose acceptance the control plane applied at a sequence the snapshot covers. A delayed snapshot can therefore not misclassify an assignment accepted after it, and a `snapshot` number orders snapshots of one journal (pass-2 finding 2). For any other omission, the only action is an idempotent re-send of an offer without a grant. Neither rule detects a journal rolled back to before an applied acceptance, which a delayed snapshot resembles (pass-3 finding 1). So the control plane sends an inventory request with a fresh `requestId` on reconnect, and only a snapshot answering the latest request proves a rollback. It is a message on the command subject, like the confirmation, because the worker may read nothing else. The inventory is a snapshot taken on every reconnect and can be rebuilt, so it is not journaled. `throughSequence` orders it after the observations it reflects. It lists assignments and executions as flat lists, with the journal state and an `uncertain` flag beside it, as the journal keeps uncertainty (pass-1 finding 2). A snapshot is split into parts of at most 256 entries of each list, because a bound on one message cannot hold a worker's whole journal under the `1 MiB` payload limit (pass-1 finding 3); the control plane acts on omissions only when it has every part. Capacity declarations and admission pressure change state the scheduler relies on, so they are sequenced observations and keep their order.
7. **Protocol version and window.** The version is an integer, so that a worker compares it without parsing. `VersionedCommand` is the part that keeps its meaning across versions. A worker rejects every command of an unsupported version, an offer included, with `execution.command.rejected`, which needs only the command's `commandId` and type, because an unknown payload guarantees no other field (pass-1 finding 1). The control plane maps an offer's `commandId` to its assignment and refuses it with `protocol-not-supported`, which ends the assignment and releases its reservation. That needs the reason in the scheduling domain's `REFUSAL_REASONS` and in the capacity contract's table, but not in the worker's wire `RefusalReason`. The control plane supports the current and the previous version. This is the author's proposal: one previous version lets workers be upgraded after the control plane without a drain.
8. **Launch authorization outcomes.** Status 200 carries every decision recorded for an `operationId`, the retryable rejections `quarantined` and `unavailable` included, because each is final for its operation (launch authorization section 6). Status 503 means no decision was reached, and the worker repeats the same `operationId`. The grant is flat, with `ttlMilliseconds` and `skewMilliseconds` as the domain's launch parameters. `reportsNotStarted` comes from the domain's `LaunchRequest`: the supervisor's launch record shows that no spawn was claimed, which launch authorization section 4 requires to authorize the same execution again. It is not the seal, after which no grant can be used (pass-1 finding 5). Only non-retryable reasons appear in `LaunchRejectionReason`, the observation's reason, because only those are reported (section 9).
9. **Task result is the domain outcome fact.** `readTaskResult` returns `pending` or the fields of `TaskOutcomeFact`, the `AttemptOutcome` with its `cancelled`, `ownershipGeneration`, and `endedAt` included, plus the task's evidence records. This keeps item 16 a serialization of the domain.
10. **Evidence.** Publication is one multipart request of the record and its content; the producer chooses `evidenceId`, so that a retry stores nothing twice. The fields follow architecture section 8, and all are required: producer, task, attempt, run, input artifacts, code revision, validator or producing program with its version, outcome, digest, and capture time (pass-1 finding 6). The producer is the authenticated worker, never a field. Content is identified by its SHA-256 digest, and download is a separate operation that never serves content that fails its digest.
11. **Admission ownership generation.** The SDLC `Admission` schema lacked the ownership generation that item 7 added to a used admission, so the wire rejected the domain record (pass-2 finding 1). It is now an optional property in all three documents that define `Admission`. Old consumers that reject unknown properties would reject such a record, so both SDLC documents declare version 0.2.0.
12. **Evidence ownership.** A publication names the ownership generation it was produced at, because the worker credential identifies the worker but not its generation for the attempt (pass-2 finding 3). Evidence of an older generation is kept for audit and marked not current.
13. **Credentials.** All three schemes are bearer schemes whose content items 13 and 16 define; enrollment returns both credentials as opaque secrets. Adding a security requirement to the SDLC operations is breaking for `sdlc.openapi.json`, so item 16 makes that change with its version.
14. **Delegation.** Capacity reservation section 7 assigns the delegation request's wire form to this item (pass-1 finding 4). `requestDelegation` names the delegating attempt and its ownership generation and the child task as SDLC admission takes it, never the parent; the control plane derives the parent and root. The outcome is an admitted child with its admission, a delegation that waits for capacity, or a rejection with the reasons that section raises at delegation and task creation. `delegation-budget-exhausted` is raised at reservation, not here. The worker chooses `delegationId`, and a repeat returns the current state, so that waiting needs no second operation.
15. **Subjects.** The addresses follow D3 (a) and the trust model's permission rule, with the installation as the first segment. Item 2 may still change them; the coordinator relays any conflict.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Item 2 may report a subject, header, or size fact that conflicts with section 2 of the contract page. Mitigation: the documents are at 0.1.0, and no consumer exists before item 12.
- Dependencies between fields are schema branches: retryability by refusal or rejection reason, pressure causes, and spawn failure (pass-2 finding 4). Comparisons between two values stay in prose, because JSON Schema cannot compare fields, such as `skew` less than `ttl` and a growing `confirmedThrough`. Items 12, 17, 20, and 21 must check them, and the contract page lists them.
- The merged vocabulary forbids two documents from using one name for different schemas. This is intended, and the generator names the conflict.

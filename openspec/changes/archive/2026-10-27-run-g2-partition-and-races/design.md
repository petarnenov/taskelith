## Context

The `make` services run PostgreSQL, NATS, and Toxiproxy, with Toxiproxy routes `postgres` and `nats`. The control plane composition root, `startControlPlane`, takes its timers and transport limits as settings, and the worker composition root, `startWorker`, takes injectable dependencies: the broker connection, the launch authority client, the launch host, the timers, the draws, and the identities. The worker has no crash seam, and there is no operator cancel API. The 24a recorder appends a result per scenario when `TASKELITH_SCENARIO_RESULTS` is set.

## Decisions

1. **One fresh installation per scenario.** `ScenarioInstallation` creates a database, a data root, and a broker account of its own, starts `startControlPlane` with short timers (placement 200 ms, lost contact 3 s, redelivery 1 s to 4 s, five deliveries), creates one run, and drops everything afterwards. No worker of another scenario is a placement candidate.
2. **Workers are the product composition.** `ScenarioWorker` calls `startWorker` with test-side dependencies: timers 20 times faster than the targets, seeded draws and identities, an ample volume, and a launch host. The worker's own modules decide everything else: intake, journal, launch orchestration, supervision protocol, and publication.
3. **Process-group launch host.** Linux without a delegated cgroup v2 subtree has no containment, so the scenario launch host uses the real record and supervisor modules on a platform without containment, as a `make verify` host is: the supervisor signals the child's process group and proves its end by `/proc` listings. Adoption on this platform reports the record unusable, so scenario g has no Linux run. The harness needs `/proc`, so the scenario files skip on hosts other than Linux and name the reason; macOS is the native acceptance's (G2 item 25).
4. **Cancellations are test-side writers.** Without an operator cancel API, a cancel is `cancelInTransaction` of the scheduling module, committed in a test transaction: without a runnable execution it ends the assignment and tells the worker; otherwise it revokes the launch and requests the attempt's cancellation. A supersession is `supersedeUngrantedInTransaction`. The epoch advance of the stale epoch scenario is a test-side update of the installation row, standing for a restore, which G2 does not implement.
5. **Ordering by gates and seeds.** `OrderedAuthority` wraps the worker's HTTP launch authority with a gate before each request leaves and before each answer returns. Each scenario draws its ordering from `TASKELITH_TEST_SEED`: heal before or after lost contact or the exit, which request reaches the authority first, the race offsets, and the restart time. A gate fails by name if it stays closed for 60 s (`Gate.wait`), each cleanup step runs within 30 s and after a failed one, and after a file's last test a guard reports, stops, and closes what a scenario left open.
6. **The crash between W1 and the ack is a process kill.** Intake acknowledges a command only after W1 commits. A worker in a child process wraps its broker connection so that acknowledging a cancellation sends `SIGKILL` to itself; the supervisor, a detached process, keeps running. The scenario restarts the worker in-process on the same root, with the same wrapper counting the acknowledgements of the redelivery.
7. **Unauthorized start from the worker's own journal.** A start that the authority did not grant is written through the worker's journal API, W2 to W5s for a new execution under the owning execution's grant, so the observation is a well-formed one of the worker's journal and outbox.
8. **Results and expected failures.** Every scenario records its result through the 24a requirements "Scenario result records" and "Opt-in scenario results file", with target `harness-composition`. A scenario that fails because of product code is a `todo` test, which runs, records `failed`, and does not fail `make verify`; `verification.md` holds its evidence. Once a defect is fixed on `dev`, its test becomes a regular test that guards the fix; B1, B2, B4, and B5 are fixed, so no `todo` test remains (`0de75d3`).

9. **Duplicate delivery is 24b's.** By the coordinator's decision, duplicate delivery is out of scope; it appears here only where a scenario needs it, as the redelivered cancellation of scenario e and the redelivered observations of the database partitions.
10. **Old-epoch exits are B4.** The owner's delegate decided (away run `20261026T164615Z-opencode-20899`) that an old-epoch exit ending its task is a G2 product defect. The stale epoch scenario passes on what the contract defines today, the rejected request and the start kept as evidence; a separate test, an expected failure until the B4 fix (#302) and a regular test since, checks that the exit changes no state.

## Risks and limitations

- The scenarios run against the `make` services, not the Compose installation; the worker is not containerized, and the launch host is the process-group one.
- The timers are scaled; a partition shorter than a target timeout may behave differently at the targets.
- The forged unauthorized start and the epoch update write state that no product path writes in G2; they stand for a defect and a restore.
- Timing under a loaded host can stretch the waits; each wait has a bound and names what it waited for.

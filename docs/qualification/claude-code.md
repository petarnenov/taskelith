### Automated use with a subscription

Consumer Terms, section 3 "Use of our Services", forbids, "Except when you are accessing our Services via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it", access "through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise". Authentication, "Generate a long-lived token", offers `claude setup-token` "For CI pipelines, scripts, or other environments where interactive browser login isn't available". Legal and compliance, "Acceptable use": "Advertised usage limits for Pro and Max plans assume ordinary, individual usage of Claude Code and the Agent SDK."

- The text says: automated access is forbidden unless explicitly permitted; the documentation appears to permit a subscriber's own scripted and CI use, and the support article on monthly API credits says Agent SDK usage signed in with a plan draws "from your plan's usage limits".
- Ambiguous: whether that permission covers a standing, multi-worker orchestrator running unattended, and whether such use is "ordinary, individual usage"; the documentation pages carry no date or version.

### Account sharing and pooled subscriptions (ADR 0005)

Consumer Terms, section 2 "Account creation and access" (EEA variant): "You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else or make your Account available to anyone else"; the US variant has the same substance in two sentences ("You also may not make your Account available to anyone else"). Section 3 forbids "bypassing any of our systems or protective measures". Usage Policy (Sep 15, 2025 version), "Do Not Abuse our Platform": "Coordinate malicious activity across multiple accounts to avoid detection or circumvent product guardrails or generating identical or similar inputs that otherwise violate our Usage Policy", and "Circumvent a ban through the use of a different account, such as the creation of a new account, use of an existing account, or providing access to a person or entity that was previously banned"; the Nov 12, 2026 version reads "Coordinate malicious activity across multiple accounts, or create multiple accounts, to avoid detection, circumvent product guardrails, or generate identical or similar inputs that otherwise violate our Usage Policy". The Nov 12, 2026 version, under "Do Not Abuse Our Platform" (accessed 2026-10-27), also forbids to "Intentionally bypass or interfere with capabilities, restrictions, or guardrails established within our products, including by circumvention of the account sign-up or log-in process or by instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or prompt injection), without prior authorization from Anthropic", and to "Resell, proxy, or otherwise provide access to Claude through unauthorized means, including services that route requests through consumer subscriptions or misrepresent the product or client being used". Legal and compliance, "Can customers offer Claude Code in their products?": "Customers may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf", and "Each end user must authenticate with their own Anthropic API key, Claude subscription plan credentials, or 3P inference provider credential"; the same section's other conditions (accessed 2026-10-27) are "The Claude Code binary must not be modified. Claude Code must be installed and run as published by Anthropic, and customers may not remove, disable, or restrict any authentication method built into it (including methods that permit signing in with a Claude account or the user's own API key)", and "That usage is billed directly to the end user under their own agreement with Anthropic or, for third-party inference providers, with the applicable provider". What is the Max plan?, "Does the Max plan have any usage limits?": "Your session-based usage limit will reset every five hours. Max plans also have a weekly usage limit that applies across all models", and "to manage capacity and ensure fair access to all users, we may limit your usage in other ways, such as weekly and monthly caps or model and feature usage, at our discretion".

- The text says: sharing an account or making it available to anyone else is forbidden, with no exception for consent; where Claude Code runs in a product or service, each end user authenticates with their own credentials; using a different account, including an existing one, to get around a ban is forbidden; no clause found names pooling or load balancing in those words, but the Nov 12, 2026 version names "services that route requests through consumer subscriptions" among the unauthorized means of providing access to Claude; in the Sep 15, 2025 version the multi-account clause is tied to malicious activity that evades detection or guardrails, while the Nov 12, 2026 version also names creating multiple accounts to avoid detection or circumvent product guardrails as a prohibition of its own; the Nov 12, 2026 version also forbids intentionally bypassing restrictions established within the products, including by circumventing "the account sign-up or log-in process", without Anthropic's prior authorization, where the Sep 15, 2025 version limits its bypass clause to instructing the model to produce harmful outputs and has no routing clause; where Claude Code runs in a product or service, the binary must stay unmodified with every built-in authentication method, including "the user's own API key", and usage is "billed directly to the end user"; a Max plan has a five-hour session limit and a weekly limit, and Anthropic may limit usage in other ways at its discretion.
- Ambiguous: whether several subscriptions held by one person count as "create multiple accounts, to avoid detection, circumvent product guardrails" under the Nov 12, 2026 version; whether rotating among one person's several subscriptions on cooldown is "bypassing any of our systems or protective measures", since usage limits are not named as such a measure, or, under the Nov 12, 2026 version, is to "bypass or interfere with capabilities, restrictions, or guardrails established within our products", whose named examples are "circumvention of the account sign-up or log-in process" and "instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or prompt injection)", not usage limits; whether a Taskelith instance whose workers run on subscription slots is one of the "services that route requests through consumer subscriptions", and whether an operator's own subscription used through it is access "through unauthorized means"; whether moving work to another slot after one slot's account is banned is circumventing "a ban"; and whether a Taskelith instance that its operator self-hosts for their own work is a customer's product whose only "end user" is that operator.

### Credential storage and intermediation by a third-party product

Legal and compliance, "Authentication and credential use": "OAuth authentication is intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans and is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic applications"; "developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens", nor "route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users"; the sentence on credentials ends (accessed 2026-10-27): "sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow". The next paragraph begins (accessed 2026-10-27): "This does not restrict how customers provision and manage their own API keys or third-party inference provider credentials — for example, configuring an API key in a development environment, secrets manager, or machine image for use by the customer's own authorized users — provided the resulting usage is billed to the key owner under their agreement with Anthropic (or the applicable provider) and is not resold or intermediated as described above"; it does not prevent "an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code as described under *Can customers offer Claude Code in their products?* above"; Anthropic "may do so without prior notice" when it enforces these restrictions. Agent SDK overview: "Anthropic does not allow third party developers to offer claude.ai login or rate limits for their products, including agents built on the Claude Agent SDK", "Unless previously approved".

- The text says: subscription OAuth is for plan purchasers and "ordinary use" of Claude Code and Anthropic's own applications; developers of products built on the SDK should use API keys and may not store or intermediate subscription credentials; sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow; customers may provision their own API keys, for example in a secrets manager or machine image, for their own authorized users, if the usage is billed to the key owner and not resold or intermediated; an end user may sign in to the unmodified binary with their own subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code, and that hosting is subject to the conditions quoted under [Account sharing and pooled subscriptions](#account-sharing-and-pooled-subscriptions-adr-0005): an unmodified binary, no removed, disabled, or restricted built-in authentication method, including "the user's own API key", and usage "billed directly to the end user"; enforcement may come without notice.
- Ambiguous: whether Taskelith, an npm package that each operator self-hosts, is a "third party developer" product; whether a stored `setup-token` token is a "session token"; whether a slot credential that the control plane stores and injects per run is a sign-in that completed "through Anthropic's own flow"; whether SDK-driven use falls under the carve-out for the unmodified binary, which names the binary and a hosting platform but not the SDK; whether workers that authenticate only through subscription slots ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-harness-subscription-account-pools.md) decision 2) "restrict any authentication method built into it", such as "the user's own API key"; whether pooled slot usage is "billed directly to the end user"; and whether unattended orchestration of that binary is "ordinary use".

### Which terms govern

Legal and compliance, "License": the Consumer Terms apply "for Free, Pro, and Max users"; "Can customers offer Claude Code in their products?" requires the Commercial Terms for "running Claude Code in your products or services (e.g. in hosted sandboxes or other agent infrastructure)". Consumer Terms (EEA): "You are a consumer if you are acting wholly or mainly outside your trade, business, craft or profession"; section 11, "Disclaimer of warranties, limitations of liability, and indemnity", under "Non-commercial use only.": "You agree that you will not use our Services for any commercial or business purposes". No equivalent clause was found in the US variant. Agent SDK overview, "License and terms" (accessed 2026-10-27): "Use of the Claude Agent SDK is governed by Anthropic's Commercial Terms of Service, including when you use it to power products and services that you make available to your own customers and end users, except to the extent a specific component or dependency is covered by a different license as indicated in that component's LICENSE file."

Consumer Terms (EEA), section 3, among the prohibited uses: "To develop any products or services that compete with our Services, including to develop or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning algorithms or models or resell the Services." Commercial Terms, D.4 "Use Restrictions": "resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic", within the clause on building a competing product.

- The text says: a Pro or Max login is governed by the Consumer Terms and the Usage Policy; both sets of terms name reselling the Services, within their clauses on competing products; the SDK license points to the same legal page; the Agent SDK overview states that use of the SDK is governed by the Commercial Terms, including in products and services offered to one's own customers and end users; in the EEA variant the user agrees not to use the Services for commercial or business purposes.
- Ambiguous: whether Taskelith is "agent infrastructure" that requires the Commercial Terms while each login stays under the Consumer Terms; which terms govern a Pro or Max login used through the Agent SDK, since the legal page applies the Consumer Terms "for Free, Pro, and Max users" and the Agent SDK overview applies the Commercial Terms to use of the SDK; which terms govern an EEA subscriber whose work is professional; and whether the non-commercial clause, placed in the liability section, restricts use or only frames the liability exclusion in the same sentence.

### Data use for training

Data usage, "Data training policy": "We will train new models using data from Free, Pro, and Max accounts when this setting is on (including when you use Claude Code from these accounts)." Consumer Terms, section 4: training applies "unless you opt out of training through your account settings", except for Feedback and material flagged for safety review. Commercial Terms: "Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content from Services".

- The text says: training on code sent through a Pro or Max login depends on each account's setting (5-year retention when on, 30-day when off); API-key use under the Commercial Terms is not used for training.
- Ambiguous: in a pool, each slot owner's setting decides the treatment of every project that slot serves, and no text addresses how a project owner could see or control it.

### Consequences for D15

The most direct clauses are on the Legal and compliance page: subscription OAuth is "intended exclusively" for plan purchasers and "ordinary use", and where Claude Code runs in a product or service, "Each end user must authenticate with their own" credentials and customers "may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage". Against the ADR 0005 pool model: slots owned by different people serving work that is not their own appear to conflict with these clauses and with "make your Account available to anyone else"; the control plane storing and injecting subscription tokens appears to conflict with "may not collect, store, or intermediate" if Taskelith is read as a third-party developer's product; least-loaded rotation across accounts is not named but may be read as bypassing protective measures or as beyond "ordinary, individual usage", and moving work to another account after a ban may be read as circumventing it. The Usage Policy effective Nov 12, 2026 also forbids to "Intentionally bypass or interfere with capabilities, restrictions, or guardrails established within our products", with "circumvention of the account sign-up or log-in process" and "instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or prompt injection)" as its named examples, and names "services that route requests through consumer subscriptions" among unauthorized means of providing access to Claude; the 2026-10-25 review omitted both clauses. ADR 0005 decision 5, as amended on 2026-10-25, keeps a session on the harness and slot that started it until its work is finished and the session and its agent are closed, so cooldown or load never moves a running session to another account; new sessions are still spread across slots by least-loaded selection. After a limit, ADR 0005 decision 4, as amended on 2026-10-25, continues the work in a new session on a different harness, or waits for the slot's cooldown, and never moves it to another account of the same harness. Unattended automated use itself appears permitted for a subscriber's own scripts, with its limit undefined.

The text treats two cases differently, and both stay ambiguous. (i) An operator who self-hosts Taskelith and uses one subscription of their own for their own work authenticates with their own credentials, which the carve-out for "signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription" appears to allow, since it extends to cases "where a platform hosts Claude Code"; such hosting is subject to the conditions that "The Claude Code binary must not be modified", that customers "may not remove, disable, or restrict any authentication method built into it (including methods that permit signing in with a Claude account or the user's own API key)", and that usage is "billed directly to the end user"; not stated is whether workers that authenticate only through subscription slots under ADR 0005 decision 2 meet the condition on authentication methods, whether SDK-managed use of the binary falls under that carve-out, whether SDK-driven unattended orchestration is "ordinary use", whether the control plane's storage of that token is a developer storing credentials, and, for an EEA subscriber, whether professional work conflicts with "Non-commercial use only". (ii) Pools of subscriptions of different people, or rotation across accounts, find no supporting text and meet the clauses above more directly; several subscriptions of one person are not addressed by name, and no clause forbids holding several plans, but the Usage Policy effective Nov 12, 2026 forbids creating multiple accounts to avoid detection or circumvent product guardrails as a prohibition of its own. Anthropic was not contacted; the legal page offers "contact sales" for questions about permitted authentication methods, and the Agent SDK overview's restriction applies "Unless previously approved".

The owner decided D15 on 2026-10-25 from the review of that day, which omitted the passages marked "(accessed 2026-10-27)" in this section, although they were on the pages then. Open question for the owner: whether D15 (a) and [ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-harness-subscription-account-pools.md) decision 2 ("Subscription authentication only", "API-key billing is not used") stand with these passages: the Usage Policy clauses on "services that route requests through consumer subscriptions" and on bypassing restrictions "including by circumvention of the account sign-up or log-in process", the Agent SDK's governance by the Commercial Terms, the hosting conditions on built-in authentication methods and on usage "billed directly to the end user", and sign-in "through Anthropic's own flow". This record states the question and makes no recommendation.

## Method

Environment: macOS 26.6.2 (build 25G83) on x86_64, Node.js 24.22.1. Times are UTC unless marked local (UTC+03:00). The SDK was installed with lifecycle scripts disabled into a scratch directory outside the repository. Every scenario ran once, in the series named `s2`, through `make all` from scripts whose SHA-256 hashes are written into each run's metadata. The final summary step of that `make all` failed on a state file in the runs directory; the summary script, which only reads the logs and is outside the hashed set, was fixed and re-run. An earlier series was superseded because its runner, sampler, and isolation were flawed; none of its results are used.

Isolation of each run:

- The harness received only an allowlisted environment: `PATH`, `HOME`, `USER`, a per-run `TMPDIR`, the dedicated `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, the proxy variables, the proxy's CA certificate, and the telemetry flags under test, plus the variables the SDK itself adds (`CLAUDE_CODE_ENTRYPOINT`, `CLAUDE_AGENT_SDK_VERSION`). `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER` was not set; see [Host side effects](#host-side-effects).
- SDK options: `settingSources: []`, `strictMcpConfig: true`, no MCP servers, only the `Bash` tool, permission mode `default`, model `claude-haiku-5-5`.
- Each run used a fresh, empty working directory.
- The probe process itself received no proxy variables, so only harness traffic passed the proxy.

Egress measurement:

- A TLS-terminating proxy, trusted by the harness through `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`, logged the method, host, and path of every request, without query strings or bodies.
- A sampler listed every TCP and UDP socket of the probe process tree every 250 ms to detect traffic that bypassed the proxy.
- Blind spots: connections shorter than the sampling interval, including those of a process that ignores the proxy; DNS lookups made by the system resolver; processes that detach from the probe's process tree; loopback connections other than to the proxy, which the summary filters (the raw socket logs show only proxy connections); request bodies, so traffic on the inference path cannot be separated by purpose; the raw harness message stream, which was not retained (correlation and session results are counters computed by the probe); and the interactive `/login` step, which ran outside the proxy and sampler.

Results:

- Telemetry is on by default and goes to Anthropic's event-logging endpoint, an evaluation endpoint, and Datadog. `DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` removes all three, which meets ADR 0003 decision 4. These paths are classified by name; request bodies were not inspected.
- With telemetry off, five configuration requests to the vendor remain. They are non-telemetry vendor traffic, acceptable until G8 under decision 10. `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC=1` reduces them to the MCP server listing.
- The same flag also removes one `POST /v1/messages` per new session (3 versus 2 for the same conversation; new sessions start with two near-simultaneous requests, resumed sessions do not). That request is non-essential traffic of unidentified purpose on the inference path; path-level logging cannot separate it from inference.
- `GET /v1/mcp_servers` appears even with `strictMcpConfig`; no MCP server was loaded.

### Pending approval across a crash

In `crash-pending`, the probe wrote the pending `Bash` request at 12:45:13.999, the orchestrator observed it at 12:45:14.226, and killed both the probe and the harness with SIGKILL at 12:45:16.278. In `crash-resume`, a new process resumed the session with the prompt "Continue.", and the probe denied any new request:

- The original request (its `toolUseID`) was not re-delivered.
- The harness issued a new request with a new `toolUseID`; in this run it checked whether the file existed. (An observation from the superseded series, which supports no result here, showed the same side effect re-planned, so the re-planned action may vary.) Any new request would run if a host approved it.
- The side effect of the original request never ran.

So pending native approvals are not durable across a crash. This matches the rule in [architecture section 7](../architecture.md#durable-chat-turns-and-provider-approvals): the plugin must reconcile the journaled request as unresolved, must never send its stored answer to a new request, and must present any new request for a fresh decision. The SDK types also document an automatic re-run of an interrupted turn controlled by `CLAUDE_CODE_RESUME_INTERRUPTED_TURN`; that was not tested.

## Host side effects

Timestamps and lock files indicate that the harness's auto-updater acted on the host-wide installation even though every run used a dedicated `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`. The cause is inferred, not observed:

- During the interactive `/login` with the pinned 2.1.308 binary (15:42:34 local), the owner's `~/.local/bin/claude` symlink to the newer 2.1.310 installation disappeared. The dedicated configuration directory gained an `.update.lock` at the same minute. The symlink was restored afterwards.
- During series `s2` (15:43:57 local, within `s2-interrupt`), `~/.local/share/claude/versions` was modified while the dedicated directory recorded a `.last-cleanup`. Whether an older installed version was removed could not be determined.
- The `s2` runs did not set `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER`. No update download appeared in their proxy logs or sampled sockets, but an updater that detaches from the process tree or ignores the proxy would be invisible to both. The egress of the `/login` step was not measured.

Consequence: a worker must disable the auto-updater (`DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1`) and must not share a home-level installation with other harness versions. Running each harness in the worker container image keeps such writes inside the container. Later qualification runs must set `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1`.

## Consequences for the agent port

These findings constrain the port contract, not the kernel. Per ADR 0003 decision 8, nothing harness-specific enters kernel or module code, types, or tests.

- Correlation: the port carries a host-generated turn ID; a plugin maps it to its native correlation field.
- Approvals: the port models native permission requests with a native request ID and a single response. After a restart, unresolved requests are reconciled, never replayed.
- Outcomes: the port defines terminal outcomes; a plugin maps native result subtypes and transport failures onto them.
- Self-update: a worker pins each harness binary, disables self-update, and checks that a harness run writes nothing outside its declared directories.
- Codex must pass the same conformance scenarios before the port is frozen (ADR 0003 consequences).

Proposal for the port design, not yet decided: each plugin manifest declares its telemetry switch and expected outbound destinations, so the egress test can be generated from the manifest.

- The automatic interrupted-turn re-run, and repeated runs to check stability.
